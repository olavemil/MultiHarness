and others have spoken since.

**First, who is the question aimed at?** A message can name someone without being addressed to
them:

- `@${mentioned_other} can you check this?` — aimed at them. Not the agent's.
- `${mentioned_other}: I pushed the fix` — from or about them. Not the agent's.
- `do you think ${mentioned_other} would agree?` — aimed at **the agent**, about them.
- `would ${mentioned_other}'s approach work here?` — aimed at **the agent**, about them.

Being named is not the same as being addressed. When the verb points at the agent — `do you
think`, `would you`, `what's your read` — the message is the agent's to answer no matter
whose name appears in it.

**If it is aimed at the agent: reply. Stop here — that is the whole decision.** A question
put directly to it does not additionally have to advance a thread or prove it is needed. It was
asked; answer it.

**Only if it is not aimed at the agent**, ask a second question: does it still carry the
agent's thread forward and want its input — for example, does it ask ${mentioned_other} to
confirm something the agent said, where the answer turns on detail only it has?

If neither, stay silent. Two other people picking up a subject the agent once raised is a
conversation it started, not one it is owed a place in.
